03

AI & GenAI trends

The trends changing cost, control, and contract terms.

AI & GenAI
TREND / 01

Agents become coworkers with their own accounts

PricingRisk

Google announced the Gemini agent on 8 Oct (private preview, no full pricing): it gets its own Workspace account, email and calendar, logs its actions and has a hard spend cap per project. Microsoft's new Copilot (25 Sep) adds Autopilot, an agent that keeps working in the background.

So what for procurement

An agent is a new kind of licensee. Contracts need terms for agent identities, spend caps, audit logs and liability.

Sources [5] [6] [7] [8]
TREND / 02

Frontier models churn every few weeks

Lock-inOpportunity

OpenAI put GPT-6 into every ChatGPT plan on 7 Oct. Google made Gemini 4 Argon generally available (13 Oct) and Meta launched Muse Wonder (12 Oct). Benchmarks disagree: a model ranked near the top overall can rank in the 30s on a legal-agent test.

So what for procurement

Avoid single-model commitments. Buy through platforms that let you switch models and test on your own tasks.

Sources [5] [6] [7] [8]
TREND / 03

Small models make high-volume AI cheap

OpportunityPricing

Claude Haiku 5.5 (7 Oct) costs $0.10 / $0.50 per million input/output tokens. Gemini 4 Argon is $2 / $10, cached inputs 95% off, temporary launch discount.

So what for procurement

Route routine work to small models. Write price step-down clauses so you benefit when list prices fall.

Sources [5] [6] [7] [8]
TREND / 04

The model is the cheap part

Pricing

Integration, compliance and monitoring cost $5–$10 for every $1 spent on models. 45% of IT leaders have low or no confidence in calculating AI costs.

So what for procurement

Build business cases on total cost of ownership, not token price.

Sources [5] [6] [7] [8]
TREND / 05

Agent safety is now a contract issue

Risk

Anthropic cut internal agent tests off from the live internet after agents misused real websites. OpenAI publishes a log of unwanted model behaviour. Microsoft's CEO called for an emergency stop a human can pull, logs the AI cannot edit, and no model grading its own work.

So what for procurement

Add human override, tamper-proof logs, permission scoping and incident-notification timelines to RFPs.

Sources [5] [6] [7] [8]
TREND / 06

Adoption up, spend cooling, chip stocks wobbling

SupplyRisk

Ramp's October index puts paid AI adoption at 55.9% of US businesses, but token spend has barely grown in two months. AI chip stocks shed over $1 trillion in the week of 12 Oct; CoreWeave's debt is under scrutiny.

So what for procurement

Check the financial health of AI and GPU-cloud suppliers; ask for data portability, escrow and step-in rights.

Strategy playbook

Eight sourcing plays. Start with the three that need action now.

Strategy playbook
P1Now

Guardrails on consumption pricing

Credits and tokens turn fixed costs variable

  • Hard spend caps; alerts at 50/80/100%; agents stop rather than bill overage
  • Pre-paid pools with rollover; fixed overage rates
  • Monthly line-item usage reporting
P2Now

Pull hardware buys forward

Memory prices rising; supply tight into 2027

  • Lock Q4/Q1 pricing on framework agreements
  • Extend healthy-device refresh by 6–12 months
  • Memory pass-through formulas in writing
P3Now

Run a VMware exit cost model

A credible alternative is the only lever that works

  • Price two alternatives incl. migration effort
  • Plan 8–12 weeks mid-size, 6+ months enterprise
  • Start before the renewal window
P4This quarter

Stay model-agnostic

The best model changes every few weeks

  • Prefer multi-model platforms
  • 12-month terms for AI-specific commitments
  • Price step-down tied to list price
P5This quarter

M&A protection in contracts

Tech M&A up 38% this year

  • Change-of-control: terminate or freeze price
  • Cap annual uplifts (CPI or 3–5%)
  • Track PE-owned and target vendors
P6This quarter

Agent terms in AI contracts

Agents act alone and can overreach

  • Human override; tamper-proof logs
  • Least-privilege permissions; incident-notification SLAs
  • No training on inputs; clear data residency
P7At renewal

Secure data and API rights

Some SaaS vendors restrict third-party agents

  • Contracted API access for any agent
  • Bulk export formats and exit assistance
P8By Q1 2027

Prepare for Malaysia's AI law

Covers AI used by Malaysian deployers, wherever hosted

  • Inventory AI systems incl. features inside SaaS
  • Ask vendors for risk assessments and incident-reporting support
  • Compliance-cooperation clauses in new contracts

Learning centre

Ten terms to know. Five questions to test your judgement.

Learn

The procurement glossary

10 terms
01Token+

A chunk of text (~¾ of a word). AI APIs price per million tokens; output costs more than input.

02Agent / agentic AI+

AI that carries out multi-step tasks on its own (sending emails, updating records) rather than just answering.

03Inference vs training+

Training builds a model; inference runs it. Enterprises mostly pay for inference.

04RAG+

Retrieval-augmented generation: the AI looks up your documents first, then answers from them.

05Small language model+

A smaller, cheaper, faster model for routine high-volume work; some run on a laptop.

06Context caching+

Reusing input the model has already processed, billed at a large discount.

07Credits+

A vendor's own metering currency (Copilot Credits, Flex Credits). Convert to cost per task to compare.

08MCP+

Model Context Protocol: an open standard for connecting AI tools to systems and data. A lock-in hedge.

09HBM+

High-bandwidth memory used in AI chips; its demand is why ordinary server/PC memory is scarce.

10Outcome-based pricing+

Paying per result (resolved ticket, processed invoice). Needs a tight contractual definition of "outcome".
